# Blog reading paths

Identify the article's intent, hub or reason for standing alone, role in its cluster, and the most specific relevant product destination. Establish candidates from pillar/subtopic, definition/application, prerequisite/advanced reading, problem/method, method/case, adjacent tutorial steps, comparison/choice, or a reader problem and a supporting product feature. Shared keywords, tags or similar titles are insufficient.

Scripts, rules and embeddings may suggest links. Review each direction in its paragraph, confirm the destination contributes distinct value and check its publication state. A prerequisite in a reading sequence is a candidate, not a document metadata dependency or evidence of a live backlink.

For a new article, evaluate both its outgoing links and natural opportunities to link to it from older articles. Edit the surrounding sentence if useful; skip a forced insertion. Use a consistent canonical destination for the same concept. Hubs explain the subject boundary and organize spokes; spokes return to the hub where useful. Tags and recommendation modules supplement these paths. Link to a specific tool, feature or use case at the point the reader needs it; a blanket homepage, registration or pricing CTA does not establish relevance.

For Markdown delivery, resolve links against the agreed batch file-to-destination mapping and identify which files the recipient must import together. For integration or live delivery, check actual availability; future targets require an explicit release gate. Revisit inbound and outbound paths when articles launch, hubs or clusters change, pages merge or disappear, URLs move or product capabilities change. Batch large reviews by family and capture date.
